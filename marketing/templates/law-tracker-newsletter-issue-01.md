## 1. Four US states, four definitions

| State | Law | Status | Definition of "neural data" | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Colorado** | HB24-1058, "Protect Privacy of Biological Data" | Signed 17 Apr 2024; effective 7 Aug 2024 | "information that is generated by the measurement of the activity of an individual's central or peripheral nervous systems and that can be processed by or with the assistance of a device." Adds biological/neural data to "sensitive data" under the Colorado Privacy Act | leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1058 (primary law, A) |
| **California** | SB 1223 (CCPA amendment) | Chaptered 28 Sep 2024, Ch. 887 | "information that is generated by measuring the activity of a consumer's central or peripheral nervous system, and that is not inferred from non-neural information." Neural data becomes "sensitive personal information" | leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, SB1223 (primary law, A) |
| **Connecticut** | SB 1295 → Public Act 25-113 | Signed 24 Jun 2025; CTDPA amendment sections headed "Effective July 1, 2026" | "any information that is generated by measuring the activity of an individual's central nervous system." Added as item (G) of "sensitive data" | cga.ct.gov bill status and PA 25-113 text (primary law, A) |
| **Montana** | SB 163 (2025) | Adopted May 2025 (per peer-reviewed review) | **Statute text not yet reviewed** | Cabrera et al., *Bioethics* 2026, doi:10.1111/bioe.70062 (A, secondary to statute) |

**The definitions do not agree.** Colorado and California cover the central **or peripheral** nervous system; California excludes data inferred from non-neural information; Connecticut covers the **central nervous system only**. On a plain reading, a peripheral signal such as EMG may fall outside Connecticut's definition while falling inside Colorado's. *Interpretation, not legal advice.*

Peer-reviewed reviews also list bills under consideration in Alabama, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Illinois and Vermont (*EMBO Rep* 2025, doi:10.1038/s44319-025-00505-6; Cabrera et al. 2026). **We have not verified the status of those bills.**

## 2. Connecticut: effective 1 July 2026
Public Act 25-113 was signed by the Governor on 24 June 2025. The sections amending the Connecticut Data Privacy Act carry "Effective July 1, 2026" headers, and they add neural data to the definition of sensitive data. That date has passed.

## 3. Montana: what we know, and what we haven't verified
A 2026 peer-reviewed review reports that Montana "adopted SB 163 in May 2025, a law aimed at protecting mental privacy via regulating specific types of neurological data", extending consumer sensitive-data protections to neurotechnology data (Cabrera et al., *Bioethics* 2026). **We have not yet reviewed the statute text itself**, so we do not summarise its definitions or obligations here. We will update this tracker when we have.

## 4. Federal: the MIND Act
"In September 2025, federal legislators introduced the Management of Individuals' Neural Data (MIND) Act of 2025." According to a peer-reviewed article in *Neurology*, it "defined 'neural data,' addressed ethical concerns about its collection and use, and directed the Federal Trade Commission to study how to regulate it" (Young, Simon & Evans, *Neurology* 2026, doi:10.1212/wnl.0000000000214942). **Bill number, sponsors and current status: not verified.** There is no federal neural-data statute in force (Cabrera et al. 2026).

## 5. EU: AI Act Article 5(1)(f)
Applicable from **2 February 2025**, Article 5(1)(f) prohibits AI systems used "to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions, except … for medical or safety reasons" (artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5, B). Related: 5(1)(a) (subliminal or manipulative techniques) and 5(1)(g) (biometric categorisation inferring sensitive traits). Separately, neural data is personal data under the GDPR, and falls under Art. 9 special categories where it reveals health or is processed to uniquely identify someone (*EMBO Rep* 2025).

## 6. What this means for data teams (general observations, not legal advice)
- One recording can be "neural data" in one state and not in another, so classification may need to happen per channel and per derived feature.
- Consent may need versioning, and withdrawal may need to reach derived data and trained models.
- Models that infer emotional or cognitive states may need use-restriction information when deployed in the EU.
