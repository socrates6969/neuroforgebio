## 1. US state neural-data laws (verified)

| State | Instrument | Status / dates | Definition of "neural data" (verbatim where opened) | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Colorado** | **HB24-1058**, "Protect Privacy of Biological Data" | **Signed 17 Apr 2024; effective 7 Aug 2024** | "information that is generated by the measurement of the activity of an individual's central or peripheral nervous systems and that can be processed by or with the assistance of a device." "Biological data" covers neural and other properties, used for identification purposes | Adds biological/neural data to "sensitive data" under the Colorado Privacy Act, which means opt-in consent rules apply to sensitive data | leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1058 (A) |
| **California** | **SB 1223** (CCPA amendment) | **Chaptered 28 Sep 2024, Ch. 887** | "information that is generated by measuring the activity of a consumer's central or peripheral nervous system, and that is not inferred from non-neural information" | Neural data becomes "sensitive personal information" under the CCPA/CPRA, with a right to limit use | leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, SB1223 (A) |
| **Montana** | **SB 163** (2025) | "adopted SB 163 in May 2025, a law aimed at protecting mental privacy via regulating specific types of neurological data" | Statute text **not opened**: legislature site is a JS app and LegiScan blocked | Per the review below, extends consumer sensitive-data protections to neurotechnology data | Cabrera et al., *Bioethics* 2026, doi:10.1111/bioe.70062, PMC12797108 (A, secondary to statute) |
| **Connecticut** | **SB 1295 → Public Act 25-113** | **Signed by the Governor 24 Jun 2025.** The CTDPA amendment sections carry "Effective July 1, 2026" headers | "'Neural data' means any information that is generated by measuring the activity of an individual's **central nervous system**." Neural data is added as item (G) of "sensitive data" | Neural data is sensitive data under the Connecticut Data Privacy Act | cga.ct.gov bill status + PA 25-113 DOCX text (A) |

**States considering bills**, per peer-reviewed reviews: Alabama, Connecticut (since enacted), Massachusetts, Minnesota, Illinois, Vermont (Ienca-type review: "Mental privacy: navigating risks, rights and regulation", *EMBO Rep* 2025, doi:10.1038/s44319-025-00505-6, PMC12287510, A; Cabrera et al. 2026, A). Status of each not verified.

### The definitions do not agree, and that is a product opportunity
- **Colorado:** central **or peripheral** nervous system, and the data must be processable by a device.
- **California:** central **or peripheral**, but it **excludes data inferred from non-neural information**.
- **Connecticut:** **central nervous system only.** On a plain reading, peripheral signals such as EMG may fall outside it. *Interpretation, not legal advice.*

One EMG or EEG recording can therefore be "neural data" in one state and not in another. Classification per jurisdiction has to live in software, at the level of each channel and each derived feature.

## 2. US federal
- **MIND Act, verified as real.** "In September 2025, federal legislators introduced the Management of Individuals' Neural Data (MIND) Act of 2025." It "defined 'neural data,' addressed ethical concerns about its collection and use, and directed the Federal Trade Commission to study how to regulate it." (Young, Simon & Evans, "Safeguarding Neural Data", *Neurology* 2026, doi:10.1212/wnl.0000000000214942, A.)
  - Bill number, sponsors and current status: **not verified** (congress.gov and senate.gov returned 403).
- **No federal neural-data statute exists.** "The federal government in the United States has not acted to regulate the development of neurotechnology" (Cabrera et al. 2026, A). Federal governance is mostly "soft policies".
- Three US Senators asked a federal agency to examine neurotech-industry practices (EMBO Rep 2025, A). The paper names the FCC; the MIND Act direction goes to the FTC. Treat the agency detail as **unverified**.

## 3. HIPAA / BAA
- HIPAA applies to **covered entities** (health plans, clearinghouses, and providers that transmit health information electronically) and their **business associates**: hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities (opened via Wayback, A).
- **Consequence:** most consumer and wellness neurotech companies, and many research-only BCI startups, are **not** HIPAA covered entities. For them, the state consumer-privacy laws above are the binding constraint. A BAA matters when the customer is a hospital or clinical-trial sponsor handling PHI.
- **HIPAA infrastructure is a commodity:**
  - AWS lists "175+" HIPAA-eligible services, including S3, Timestream and SageMaker AI, under an AWS BAA (AWS HIPAA reference, updated 3 Sep 2026, D).
  - Aptible includes HIPAA on its **$499/mo** Production tier (aptible.com/pricing, D).
  - Weights & Biases offers HIPAA on its Enterprise plan (wandb.ai/site/pricing, D).

## 4. FDA: where software becomes a device
- **Device software functions policy.** Software functions fall into three groups (FDA "Device Software Functions Including Mobile Medical Applications" page, A):
  - (a) device functions under oversight;
  - (b) functions under **enforcement discretion**, such as helping patients self-manage without specific treatment suggestions, or automating simple tasks for providers;
  - (c) **non-device** functions.
  - The page cites **21st Century Cures Act §3060** (which removed certain software functions from the §201(h) device definition), the "Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications" guidance (2013, updated 2015/2019/2022) and the **Clinical Decision Support Software** final guidance (Sep 2022).
- **Implanted BCI guidance.** "Implanted Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) Devices for Patients with Paralysis or Amputation – Non-clinical Testing and Clinical Considerations", **issued 20 May 2021** (draft 22 Feb 2019). An update note says implanted BCIs "should generally address the recommendations for an **Enhanced Documentation Level**" under the 14 Jun 2023 final guidance "Content of Premarket Submissions for Device Software Functions". The guidance points to FDA's cybersecurity and off-the-shelf software guidances (fda.gov/media/120362/download, A).
- **Product boundary for us (planning assumption, not legal advice):**
  - A data platform that stores, converts, cleans and audits neural data **without diagnosing or recommending treatment** is designed to stay a non-device function.
  - A "motor intent" or "cognitive state" model marketplace whose outputs drive a clinical or assistive BCI is **likely device software** in the customer's product. We would then be a supplier of OTS/SOUP components, and the customer carries the 510(k), De Novo or PMA burden.
  - Marketing must avoid medical claims (BRIEF rule).
- **Science-safety flag:** any clinical or human-stimulation work needs IRB approval and, where applicable, FDA IDE (the IDE route is referenced in the BCI guidance).

## 5. EU
- **GDPR.** Neural data is personal data. Where it reveals health, or is processed to uniquely identify someone, it falls under Art. 9 special categories. The review literature states that GDPR "already offer[s] stronger safeguards for neural data than those in many other jurisdictions" (*EMBO Rep* 2025, A).
- **EU AI Act, Art. 5 prohibitions, applicable from 2 Feb 2025** (artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5, B):
  - **5(1)(f)** bans AI "to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of **workplace and education** institutions, except … for medical or safety reasons".
  - **5(1)(a)** bans subliminal or manipulative techniques.
  - **5(1)(g)** bans biometric categorisation that infers sensitive traits.
  - Recital 44 targets emotion inference "on the basis of their biometric data".
  - **Direct product impact:** a "cognitive state" or emotion model sold into EU workplace or education settings is prohibited unless used for medical or safety reasons. Marketplace listings need a use-restriction flag.
- **Latin America (context):** Chile's 2021 constitutional amendment protects "cerebral activity and the information drawn from it". In 2023 Chile's Supreme Court ordered neural-data deletion. Rio Grande do Sul (Brazil) passed a similar amendment in 2023; bills exist in Ecuador, Colombia, Mexico and Uruguay (*EMBO Rep* 2025, A).

## 6. Compliance requirements the product must support (derived)
1. Per-jurisdiction classification of every channel and derived feature (CO/CA/CT/MT definitions differ).
2. Opt-in consent capture with versioning. Consent withdrawal must propagate deletion to raw data, derivatives and trained models (the lineage problem).
3. A "limit use" flag for California sensitive personal information.
4. Use-restriction metadata on models (EU AI Act 5(1)(f)).
5. BAA-capable deployment for clinical customers. Use commodity cloud; do not market this as a moat.
6. FDA-facing: software documentation (Enhanced Documentation Level), cybersecurity, OTS/SOUP disclosure for model components.
