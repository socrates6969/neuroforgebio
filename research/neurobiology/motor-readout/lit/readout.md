## 1. Information theory and neural channel limits

|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Shannon CE. A mathematical theory of communication. Bell Syst Tech J 27:379-423, 623-656 (1948). Opened: Harvard mirror PDF people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf | PDF | C (theory, foundational) | Theorem 11 (noisy-channel coding: if H <= C, transmission with arbitrarily small error is possible; no code gives equivocation below H - C). Theorem 17: "The capacity of a channel of band W perturbed by white thermal noise power N when the average transmitter power is limited to P is given by C = W log (P+N)/N". | R2 | Borst A, Theunissen FE. Information theory and neural coding. | R3 | Dimitrov AG, Miller JP. Neural coding and decoding: communication channels and quantization. Network 2001. doi:10.1080/net.12.4.441.472, PMID 11762899 | abs | B | Models a neural system as a communication channel and quantises responses to a small reproduction set under an information distortion. It shows that estimating the full stimulus/response relation is data-hungry. **Note:** this is NOT a "bits per trial" critique of BCI ITR. The brief's "Dimitrov / bits-per-trial critique" could not be identified in any opened source: UNVERIFIED. |

## 2. Wolpaw ITR and its known flaws

| ID | Source | Read | Grade | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R5 | Wolpaw JR, Ramoser H, McFarland DJ, Pfurtscheller G. EEG-based communication: improved accuracy by response verification. IEEE Trans Rehabil Eng 1998. doi:10.1109/86.712231, PMID 9749910 | abs | B | The origin of the ITR (Thompson R8 cites this as its ref. 40). The formula is not in the abstract; its exact form was checked in R8's full text. |
| R6 | Wolpaw JR, Birbaumer N, McFarland DJ, Pfurtscheller G, Vaughan TM. Brain-computer interfaces for communication and control. Clin Neurophysiol 2002. doi:10.1016/s1388-2457(02)00057-3, PMID 12048038 | abs | B | The standard BCI review. Signals include SCPs, P300, mu/beta rhythms and intracortical activity. |
| R7 | Yuan P, Gao X, Allison B, Wang Y, Bin G, Gao S. A study of the existing problems of estimating the information transfer rate in online BCIs. J Neural Eng 2013. doi:10.1088/1741-2560/10/2/026014, PMID 23448963 | abs | B | ITR is "often reported differently, even incorrectly". It summarises the preconditions of Wolpaw's ITR, lists incorrect ITR calculations and gives online-testing guidelines. |
| R8 | Thompson DE, Quitadamo LR, Mainardi L, et al. Performance measurement for brain-computer or brain-machine interfaces: a tutorial. J Neural Eng 2014. doi:10.1088/1741-2560/11/3/035001, PMC4185283 | FT | B (consensus tutorial) | Eq. 1: B = (1/c)[log2 N + P log2 P + (1-P) log2((1-P)/(N-1))], where c = time per selection. "ITR is only equivalent to mutual information under": (i) a memoryless, stable discrete channel; (ii) equiprobable commands; (iii) equal accuracy for all targets; (iv) errors spread equally over the other symbols. "BCI systems typically violate several of these assumptions." Report ITR only when full MI is impossible, and do not optimise on it. Correlation is scale-invariant (a caveat). Fitts's-law throughput (ISO 9241) is the recommended continuous-task bit rate. |
| R9 | Speier W, Arnold C, Pouratian N. Evaluating true BCI communication rate through mutual information and language models. PLoS One 2013. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0078432, PMID 24167623 | abs | B | The ITR assumes equiprobable characters, memoryless selection and random errors. An MI metric with priors and systematic errors changed the conclusions of 2 of 11 re-evaluated studies. |
